Mimac vs Screens 5

Screens is the polished VNC client for Apple platforms, on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Vision Pro. Mimac is not a VNC client; it runs its own pipeline from the Mac's GPU to the iPad's display. That single difference explains most of the table.

Where Screens 5 is a better choice

Pick Screens if you want the same app on your iPhone and Vision Pro as well as your iPad and Mac. Pick it if you connect to any VNC server, whatever operating system it runs, or to an Intel Mac. Screens has been refined for many years, its Screens Connect relay handles the networking for you, and $29.99 a year is a modest price for a tool you use occasionally.

Where Mimac is a better choice

Pick Mimac if the job is a Mac with Apple silicon driven from an iPad Pro for real work. VNC mirrors whatever display the Mac has, which for a headless Mac means a dummy plug and a resolution chosen for the plug rather than for your iPad. Mimac creates a display at your iPad's exact resolution and streams it as video at 120 fps, with lossless audio and your microphone going the other way. Gestures behave like a MacBook trackpad. And a lifetime of Mimac is $49.99, not $179.99.

Bottom line

Screens is the better universal remote. Mimac is the better display for a Mac mini. If you reach many machines from many devices, Screens; if you sit down at one Mac from one iPad, Mimac.
