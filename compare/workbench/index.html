Where Workbench is a better choice

Pick Workbench if you draw: it supports Apple Pencil with pressure, which Mimac does not. Pick it if you want zero-configuration access from anywhere through a vendor relay, without installing Tailscale on both devices. The free 20 minutes a day is a real option for occasional check-ins, and Astropad has been shipping Mac-and-iPad display products for a decade, so there is an organisation and a support team behind it.

Where Mimac is a better choice

Pick Mimac if you would rather pay once: $49.99 is less than one year of Workbench and it never renews. Pick it if you want the numbers stated: your Mac renders at your iPad's exact resolution and the pipeline runs at 120 fps on your network, and we say so because every stage of the pipeline is pinned to 120. Pick it if you care where your screen goes: Mimac has no relay and no account; video travels between your own two devices only. And pick it for the small things that add up on a headless Mac: a rectangle dragged on the glass lands on the Mac's clipboard, your iPad's microphone works with any Mac dictation app rather than one built-in engine, and the host can start before anyone logs in.

Bottom line

Two modern products aimed at the same buyer. Workbench trades a subscription for convenience and Pencil support. Mimac trades a one-time price and a private, direct path for the requirement to run Tailscale away from home. If you already have Tailscale, or your Mac and iPad mostly share a network, the trade is easy.
