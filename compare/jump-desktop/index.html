Mimac vs Jump Desktop

Jump Desktop is the long-standing generalist: one app for RDP, VNC and its own Fluid protocol, on almost any device. Mimac does one thing, iPad Pro to Apple silicon Mac, and goes much deeper on it. Here is how they line up.

Where Jump Desktop is a better choice

Pick Jump if you need to reach Windows PCs or anything else over RDP or VNC, if you want an iPhone client, if your Mac is an Intel model, or if you would rather not run Tailscale and prefer a vendor relay that just works after signing in. Jump is also one purchase at $14.99, and it has a long track record. For a mixed fleet of machines, it is the sensible generalist.

Where Mimac is a better choice

Pick Mimac if the setup is specifically an iPad Pro with a Magic Keyboard and an Apple silicon Mac, and you use it for hours rather than minutes. The differences you will feel every day: your Mac renders at the iPad's exact resolution instead of 1080p, so text is sharp; motion runs at 120 fps instead of 60; pinch and scroll behave like a MacBook trackpad; audio is lossless; your iPad's microphone reaches dictation apps on the Mac; and a dragged rectangle lands on the Mac's clipboard. There is no account and no relay in the path. Jump's iPad app has not been updated since 2023; Mimac is built for iPadOS 26.

Bottom line

Different tools. Jump is the multi-tool; Mimac is the dedicated one. If your Mac is the only machine you reach from your iPad, the dedicated tool wins on every axis that matters at the desk.
